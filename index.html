Project Overview

Reimagining prenatal care through connected AI research

The Pregnancy Support System is a SLIIT final-year undergraduate research project that investigates how four complementary AI technologies can improve the prenatal care experience for expectant mothers and their clinicians in Sri Lanka.

The Challenge

Prenatal care in Sri Lanka, as in many developing contexts, faces a critical challenge: the gap between periodic clinical visits and continuous, personalized maternal support. Expectant mothers often navigate complex health decisions without timely, accessible guidance.

CTG printouts go uninterpreted for days. Nutrition records are burdensome. Emotional health is under-monitored. Language barriers limit engagement with health information. And clinicians lack real-time integrated insight into patient wellbeing between appointments.

This research explores whether a cohesive AI-assisted mobile system — designed around actual maternal and clinical workflows — can meaningfully bridge these gaps.

Research Scope: This project is an academic research prototype. All findings, evaluations, and conclusions are preliminary. This system does not replace professional medical advice, clinical diagnosis, or emergency healthcare services.

Research Context & Academic Foundation

A comprehensive review of the research landscape, problem definition, objectives, and methodological approach underpinning the Pregnancy Support System.

Literature Survey

Background & Prior Research

CTG Analysis & AI Interpretation

Cardiotocography (CTG) is the standard clinical tool for assessing fetal wellbeing during the antenatal period. Traditional CTG interpretation is highly subjective and dependent on expert knowledge, with reported inter-observer variability as a recognized clinical concern. Research into AI-assisted CTG interpretation has explored image-based classification using convolutional neural networks and signal-based feature extraction using classical machine learning models. Studies utilizing the CTU-CHB Intrapartum CTG dataset have demonstrated that ensemble and deep learning approaches can achieve competitive classification performance for fetal states. Key limitations in the literature include reliance on structured signal input and limited work on consumer-grade photograph digitization of printed CTG graphs — a practical scenario in low-resource healthcare settings.

Maternal Risk Classification & Monitoring

The classification of maternal health risk during pregnancy has been approached through both population-level statistical models and individual real-time machine learning classifiers. Research has demonstrated the utility of combining biometric parameters — including blood pressure, heart rate, blood glucose and gestational age — into a unified risk score. Random Forest, XGBoost and Support Vector Machine models have been applied to maternal health datasets (including the UCI Maternal Health Risk Dataset) with accuracy metrics reported in the 85–92% range in published literature. Explainability of ML predictions through SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values has emerged as a critical design principle to maintain clinician trust in AI-generated risk assessments.

Conversational AI & Emotional Wellbeing

Prenatal mental health is significantly underserved in many healthcare systems. Studies indicate that antenatal depression affects a substantial proportion of expectant mothers, yet is rarely screened for in clinical settings. Conversational AI systems, particularly transformer-based chatbots, have been explored as accessible first-line emotional support tools. Sentiment analysis and affective computing research has demonstrated the feasibility of detecting emotional states from text and, with greater uncertainty, from facial expressions. Responsible AI design for emotional contexts requires clear system limitations disclosures, avoiding diagnostic claims, and maintaining user trust through transparency. Bilingual NLP for under-resourced languages such as Sinhala presents additional complexity, with transfer learning from multilingual models (XLM-R, mBERT) showing promising results.

Prenatal Nutrition, GDM Management & Food Recognition

Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) affects a significant proportion of pregnancies globally and is associated with adverse maternal and neonatal outcomes. Dietary management, particularly glycemic load control, is a first-line intervention. Mobile technology has been explored as a tool for dietary self-monitoring, with research demonstrating improved compliance with dietary goals when food logging friction is reduced. YOLOv8 and predecessor architectures have achieved state-of-the-art performance in food detection tasks. Nutrition label OCR using established OCR frameworks has been applied to automated food database population. The cultural specificity of Sri Lankan dietary patterns — including rice-based staples and unique spice/herb profiles — necessitates a localized food recognition and nutrition database approach beyond standard Western-oriented food APIs.

Research Gap

Identified Gaps in Existing Literature & Practice

The following gaps are observed from a review of existing literature and local healthcare context, as articulated in the component research proposals. These are observations, not universal scientifically validated claims.

Gap Area Existing Solutions This Research's Contribution
CTG Interpretation Accessibility
Component 01		 Existing AI CTG tools require structured digital signal input. Consumer-grade photograph input of printed CTG graphs is understudied. Doctor review integration and shared result history are rarely combined in a single mobile-accessible tool. Proposed pipeline combining photo-based CTG digitization, AI classification (Normal / Mild / High Risk), and bidirectional doctor review — accessible on consumer mobile hardware without clinical-grade signal hardware.
Combined Risk Classification + Explainability + Alerts
Component 02		 Existing maternal risk tools often operate on single metrics or lack explainability layers. Automated real-time doctor notification when thresholds are crossed is not a standard feature in academic prototype systems. A four-tier (LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH/VERY HIGH) combined risk classifier using multiple biometrics + CTG output, SHAP-based explanation per patient, and automated FCM notification to clinician — proposed as a unified subsystem.
Culturally Appropriate Bilingual Emotional Support
Component 03		 Most AI chatbot wellbeing tools are English-only. Sinhala NLP resources are limited. Emotional support tools rarely combine sentiment analysis with adaptive response generation and mood context. Privacy-preserving on-device processing for mood inference is an emerging research area. A Sinhala-English chatbot with NLP-based sentiment analysis, optional opt-in mood context (with user-controlled camera access), calming content recommendations, and offline-capable chat history — grounded in responsible AI and privacy principles.
Multimodal Sri Lankan Dietary Support + GDM Guidance
Component 04		 General food logging apps lack Sri Lankan food databases. GDM-specific dietary guidance in local context is not available in mobile form. Pre-consumption glycemic load estimation before a meal is eaten is an understudied approach. Combined voice, image and OCR food logging reduces entry friction in ways not addressed by existing apps. YOLOv8-based Sri Lankan food recognition, nutrition label OCR, pre-consumption GDM risk scoring using Glycemic Load + maternal glucose context, "Virtual Chef" recipe generation, Sinhala voice logging, and gamified engagement — proposed as an integrated nutritional support system.
Integrated Cross-Component System
All Components		 Existing research tends to address maternal health challenges in isolation — either nutrition OR mental health OR monitoring OR CTG analysis. Cross-component data sharing (e.g. CTG risk informing overall maternal risk; nutritional context informing risk profile) is rare in academic prototypes. A coordinated four-component system where CTG analysis informs maternal risk profiling; risk levels inform clinician alerting; emotional wellbeing provides a complementary pathway; and the nutritional module uses risk and mood context for individualized guidance.
Research Problem

The Problem We Are Investigating

Expectant mothers in Sri Lanka, as in many developing healthcare contexts, face a fragmented prenatal care experience. The standard model of periodic clinical visits creates significant gaps in continuity of care, during which changes in maternal health status — whether in biometrics, fetal activity, emotional wellbeing, or dietary adherence — may go undetected or unaddressed until the next scheduled appointment.

🩺

Clinical Gaps

CTG printouts may remain uninterpreted between visits. Doctors lack real-time insight into patient biometrics and symptom progression outside clinical appointments. There is no standardized digital channel for patients to flag concerns and receive timely, clinically appropriate responses.

💬

Support Gaps

Emotional health during pregnancy is significantly under-monitored. Language barriers limit engagement with health information for Sinhala-speaking mothers. Culturally appropriate, accessible mental wellbeing resources are scarce in digital form.

🥗

Nutrition Gaps

Dietary recording is burdensome and non-compliant in practice. GDM-specific dietary guidance is not available in mobile form for the Sri Lankan dietary context. Pre-consumption risk awareness for high-glycemic-index foods is not commonly offered to at-risk mothers.

📱

Technology Gaps

Existing health apps do not integrate CTG data, biometric risk, emotional wellbeing, and nutritional context into a single coherent system. There is no cross-validated mobile tool designed for the Sri Lankan prenatal care context that addresses all four dimensions simultaneously.

Research Objectives

What This Research Sets Out to Achieve

Overall Research Objective

To design, develop and evaluate an integrated AI-assisted mobile support system for expectant mothers and clinicians in Sri Lanka, encompassing CTG-based fetal wellbeing assessment, maternal health risk classification, bilingual emotional wellbeing support, and personalized nutritional guidance — with the aim of improving continuity of prenatal care and reducing clinician workload through intelligent automation and timely communication.

Component 01 Objectives

CTG Scan Interpretation

  • Develop a pipeline for digitizing consumer-photograph CTG graphs into structured signal data using OpenCV preprocessing
  • Train and evaluate ML/DL classifiers to categorize CTG input as Normal, Mild Risk, or High Risk
  • Design a doctor review and annotated feedback flow accessible within the mobile application
  • Evaluate classification accuracy and usability of CTG result presentation 🎯 Proposed Target: >90% accuracy
Component 02 Objectives

Maternal Risk Classification

  • Build a multi-input maternal risk classifier (LOW/MEDIUM/HIGH/VERY HIGH) using RF, XGBoost and SVM model comparison
  • Implement SHAP-based explainability to surface the key contributing metrics for each risk classification
  • Develop an automated push-notification alert (FCM) to clinicians when HIGH or VERY HIGH risk is detected
  • Create a longitudinal baseline tracking system for individual maternal health deviation monitoring
Component 03 Objectives

Bilingual Chatbot & Wellbeing

  • Develop a Sinhala-English conversational AI providing culturally appropriate prenatal emotional support
  • Implement NLP-based sentiment analysis to adapt chatbot tone and content to inferred emotional state
  • Investigate optional, opt-in on-device facial expression analysis for supplementary mood context — with full user consent and local processing
  • Evaluate user-perceived emotional appropriateness, language accuracy and privacy comfort of the system
Component 04 Objectives

Virtual Nutritionist & GDM Engine

  • Develop a multimodal food logging system using voice input, image recognition (YOLOv8) and nutrition label OCR
  • Build a pre-consumption GDM risk scoring model incorporating Glycemic Load and maternal glucose context
  • Create a "Virtual Chef" recipe generator using Gemini API, personalized to available ingredients and nutritional goals
  • Implement gamification mechanics to improve dietary self-monitoring compliance and user engagement
Methodology

Research Approach & Development Process

The project follows a Design Science Research (DSR) methodology, adapted for multi-component AI healthcare system development.

1. Problem Identification & Literature Review

Formal articulation of prenatal care gaps in the Sri Lankan context; systematic review of CTG AI, risk classification, NLP and nutrition AI literature; identification of research opportunities and ethical constraints.

2. Requirements & Dataset Analysis

Elicitation of functional and non-functional requirements from literature and target user context; identification of relevant datasets (UCI Maternal Risk, CTU-CHB CTG, custom food databases); assessment of existing mobile health UX patterns.

3. System Design

Architecture design for the four-component system and their data interfaces; UI/UX wireframing for mother and doctor experiences; data schema design; proposed privacy-by-design and consent frameworks per component.

4. AI Model Development & Training

Data preprocessing, feature engineering and model training per component; CTG image processing pipeline; multi-class risk classifier training and comparison; NLP model selection and fine-tuning for bilingual support; YOLOv8 fine-tuning for local food recognition.

5. Mobile Application Implementation

Frontend mobile application development integrating all four modules; API development connecting ML backends to mobile frontends; push notification integration (FCM); authentication and data management (Firebase Firestore).

6. Evaluation & Validation

Model evaluation: accuracy, precision, recall, F1 per component; system usability evaluation (SUS) with relevant user groups; ethical review of data handling practices; comparison against proposed targets. All evaluation is academic research evaluation, not clinical validation.

Ethics & Responsible AI: All data collection proposed in this research will require informed participant consent. Identifiable health data will be anonymized. The optional facial expression mood inference feature (Component 03) requires explicit affirmative opt-in per session, a visible active-camera indicator during use, and simple one-tap disable. Processing is proposed to occur on-device. No participant health records are reproduced in this website. Proposed ethics review will be sought through institutional channels before data collection — ethics approval has not yet been granted.

Component Detail

In-depth Research Contributions

COMPONENT 01

AI-Assisted CTG Scan Interpretation

Researcher: Thirimanne D.P.B. · IT22272386

This component addresses the accessibility gap in CTG (cardiotocography) interpretation. Standard CTG monitoring is a cornerstone of antenatal care, yet the resulting printouts frequently require specialist interpretation that is not immediately available — particularly in settings where patients travel home with printed reports.

The proposed system allows a mother to photograph a printed CTG graph using their smartphone camera, or to manually enter key CTG parameters. An image preprocessing pipeline (OpenCV-based) extracts the fetal heart rate trace and uterine contraction pattern from the photograph, enabling AI-based classification.

Research Highlights

Photo-based CTG digitization using image preprocessing — under-studied in mobile health research
Three-tier classification: Normal / Mild Risk / High Risk (distinct from Component 02's four-tier maternal risk)
Feature extraction: FHR baseline, variability, accelerations, decelerations, uterine contractions
Doctor review workflow: annotate, correct or confirm AI result; add clinical notes
CTG history accessible to both mother and assigned clinician
🔬 Proposed / Under Development
This component provides clinical decision support, not autonomous fetal diagnosis. All AI outputs require clinician review before clinical action is taken.

COMPONENT 02

AI Maternal Health Analytics & Risk Classification

Researcher: Randinu P.V.K.V. · IT22557506

This component integrates multiple maternal health inputs — including heart rate, blood pressure, blood glucose, gestational age, and the CTG risk output from Component 01 — into a unified four-tier maternal risk classification system. The goal is to provide clinicians and mothers with a continuously updated, explainable risk picture that bridges the gap between clinical visits.

The system proposes a daily individualized symptom questionnaire covering fetal movement, pain levels, sleep quality, oedema and general wellbeing. Responses are combined with biometrics to detect deviations from each patient's established personal baseline — enabling early detection of concerning trends before they reach clinical threshold.

Four-Tier Risk Classification

LOW
All metrics stable
MEDIUM
Monitor closely
HIGH
Doctor alerted
VERY HIGH
Urgent alert
Model comparison: Random Forest, XGBoost, SVM — best performer selected per evaluation
SHAP-value explainability: surfaces which metrics most contributed to the risk classification
Automated FCM push notification to clinician for HIGH and VERY HIGH risk
Personal baseline deviation tracking for longitudinal health surveillance
🎯 Target Accuracy >90% on evaluation dataset; notification latency <30 seconds

COMPONENT 03

Bilingual AI Chatbot & Emotional Wellbeing

Researcher: Livera S.D. · IT22638168

Prenatal emotional wellbeing is a critical but frequently under-addressed dimension of maternal health. This component proposes an AI-powered conversational interface that supports expectant mothers in Sinhala and English — providing an accessible, non-judgmental space for emotional expression and appropriate support responses.

The chatbot uses NLP-based sentiment analysis to detect positive, neutral or distressed emotional states from conversation text, and adapts its response tone and content accordingly. Calming music, breathing exercise suggestions, and gentle positive affirmations can be recommended based on inferred context.

Key Features

Sinhala and English bilingual conversation support
NLP sentiment analysis driving adaptive response generation
Calming music recommendations and breathing/relaxation suggestions
Offline saved chat history accessible without network connection
Optional on-device facial expression mood context (see ethics note below)
Privacy & Ethics (Facial Expression Feature): The proposed on-device facial expression analysis is a research investigation only. Any implementation requires: (1) explicit user opt-in per session, (2) a visible active-camera indicator during use, (3) one-tap camera disable at any time, (4) on-device-only processing (no frame upload), and (5) clear communication that this provides supplementary mood context — not clinical mental health diagnosis. This feature is proposed; privacy safeguards are design requirements, not yet verified implementation.

COMPONENT 04

Multimodal Virtual Nutritionist & GDM Action Engine

Researcher: Nuwantha P.A.T. · IT22284716

This component proposes a comprehensive AI-driven nutritional support system for expectant mothers, with specific focus on Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) management and the Sri Lankan dietary context. It addresses the primary barrier to dietary self-monitoring — friction — through three complementary input modalities: voice, camera, and manual entry.

A key research novelty is the pre-consumption GDM risk scoring concept: before a mother eats a meal, the system can estimate the glycemic load impact using the identified food items and her current glucose context, offering a proactive rather than reactive approach to dietary management.

Comprehensive Feature Set

Voice-assisted food logging in Sinhala and English — low-friction natural language input
Image-based food recognition using YOLOv8, trained on a Sri Lankan food dataset
Nutrition label OCR for packaged/unknown items, auto-populating nutritional data
Pre-consumption GDM Risk Score using Glycemic Load + current maternal glucose context
Nutrient tracking: iron, calcium, folate — with deficiency-supportive meal ideas
"Virtual Chef": Gemini API-powered recipe generation from available home ingredients + nutritional goals
Context-aware hydration nudges informed by weather data and dietary intake
Post-meal walking goals with phone sensor completion tracking (research recommendations, subject to medical clearance)
Gamification: health points, achievements, streaks to encourage sustained engagement
Collaborative food knowledge base that learns from new product label scans, with verification step
🔬 Proposed / Under Development
GDM risk scoring and walking recommendations are research-supported guidance tools, not medical prescriptions. Individual dietary and exercise choices during pregnancy must be made in consultation with a qualified healthcare provider.

Pregnancy Support System
SLIIT · R26-IT-146

A SLIIT final-year research project investigating AI-assisted prenatal support for expectant mothers and clinicians through four integrated research components.

This is an academic research prototype. It is not a validated clinical system and does not replace professional medical advice, diagnosis, clinical judgment, or emergency healthcare services.
