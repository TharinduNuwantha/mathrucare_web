MathruCare — Integrated Maternal & Clinical System Interface

Research Background

Bridging the antenatal care divide in Sri Lanka

Investigating how four coordinated artificial intelligence technologies can enhance continuity of care, empower mothers, and streamline clinician workflows.

Contextual setting: Antenatal support extending beyond the hospital consultation room.

The Clinical & Community Challenge

In Sri Lanka, public healthcare achieves high antenatal clinic attendance, yet significant vulnerabilities emerge in the intervals between scheduled appointments. When patients return home with paper CTG traces, unusual biometric symptoms, or elevated blood glucose readings, timely professional consultation is often hindered by geographical distance and clinic congestion.

Furthermore, prenatal emotional health remains under-screened due to cultural stigma, and nutritional self-monitoring lacks tools tailored to local staple diets and Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) glycemic dynamics.

This research evaluates whether an interconnected mobile architecture — unifying photo-based CTG digitization, multimodal biometric risk modeling, Sinhala-English affective dialog, and localized nutritional computer vision — can provide a viable decision-support bridge.

Academic Boundary: This system is strictly an academic research prototype developed under SLIIT guidelines. It provides clinical decision support and maternal self-reflection; it does not replace professional medical diagnosis, obstetrical treatment, or emergency intervention.

Literature Survey

Prior Art & Scholarly Foundations

Investigating the convergence of artificial intelligence, clinical obstetrics, and maternal healthcare informatics across four distinct domains.

1. Cardiotocography (CTG) & Automated Interpretation

Cardiotocography remains the worldwide clinical benchmark for monitoring fetal heart rate (FHR) and uterine contractions (UC). However, FIGO consensus guidelines (Ayres-de-Campos et al., 2015) consistently emphasize that visual interpretation suffers from pronounced inter-observer and intra-observer variability. While automated feature extraction utilizing machine learning on the CTU-CHB Intrapartum dataset has demonstrated strong diagnostic utility, prior research overwhelmingly presumes access to structured digital time-series files. The challenge of digitizing physical thermal-paper printouts captured via smartphone photography in resource-constrained community settings remains largely under-addressed in peer-reviewed literature.

2. Multimodal Maternal Risk Modeling & Explainability

Maternal morbidity prediction models have traditionally relied on discrete physiological measurements such as blood pressure, pulse rate, and blood sugar (Ahmed et al., 2020). While tree-based ensembles (Random Forest, XGBoost) routinely achieve benchmark accuracies between 85% and 92% on public registries, clinical adoption has stalled due to the "black-box" dilemma. Incorporating SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) values is essential to provide obstetrical clinicians with interpretable reasoning for each patient's risk category, establishing clinical trust.

3. Bilingual Affective Computing & Perinatal Mental Health

Depression and anxiety during pregnancy are important maternal health concerns, yet emotional distress may go unrecognized because of social stigma and limited access to mental health services. Advances in multilingual language representation learning provide opportunities to develop conversational systems that support languages such as Sinhala and English. This research component integrates bilingual conversational sentiment analysis with facial expression-based mood estimation to support empathetic, culturally sensitive interactions during pregnancy. The system is designed to provide non-diagnostic emotional support, adapt conversational responses to estimated emotional states, and prioritize informed consent, privacy protection, and appropriate referral to professional care when needed.

4. Computer Vision in Dietary Healthcare & GDM Management

Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) necessitates stringent glycemic control to avoid macrosomia and preeclampsia (ADA, 2023). While mobile self-monitoring improves compliance, conventional logging incurs high cognitive friction. Object detection architectures (YOLOv8; Redmon et al.) show outstanding speed in food classification, yet existing models reflect Western diets. A localized Sri Lankan food dataset combined with pre-consumption glycemic load (GL) computation represents a vital research opportunity to proactively guide maternal dietary habits.

Problem Definition

The Challenge of Fragmented Antenatal Care

In the Sri Lankan healthcare context, maternal health outcomes are fundamentally challenged by the fragmentation of care between clinical consultations. While pregnant mothers receive expert care during scheduled hospital visits, the weeks between visits represent a silent gap during which emerging complications often go unnoticed.

1. Clinical Interpretation Delays

CTG printouts frequently sit unanalyzed at home until the next scheduled visit. Expectant mothers cannot distinguish normal baseline variability from subtle decelerations indicating fetal hypoxia.

2. Silent Biometric Escalation

Hypertensive spikes and blood glucose deviations develop gradually. Without continuous risk modeling, patients do not realize when combined biometric shifts warrant early clinical contact.

3. Linguistic & Emotional Isolation

Expectant mothers in Sri Lanka may experience antenatal stress without access to affordable, stigma-free emotional support in their preferred language, particularly Sinhala. In addition, facial emotion recognition models may misinterpret expressions among Sri Lankan users because of differences in cultural expression, dataset representation, lighting conditions, camera quality, and facial positioning. These inaccuracies can lead to incorrect mood predictions and inappropriate chatbot responses, highlighting the need for culturally evaluated emotion detection, consent-based camera use, and accessible Sinhala-English conversational support.

4. High Friction in Dietary GDM Tracking

Manually recording food intake in paper journals is cumbersome and non-compliant. GDM mothers consume high-glycemic carbohydrates without realizing the glycemic load until post-prandial symptoms appear.

Research Objectives

What This Study Sets Out to Accomplish

Establishing measurable scientific objectives across all four contributing components.

Overarching Research Goal

To conceptualize, develop, and empirically evaluate an integrated AI-assisted mobile decision-support system for expectant mothers and clinicians in Sri Lanka, uniting cardiotocography scan digitization, explainable maternal risk classification, bilingual affective support, and localized GDM nutrition management into a cohesive, ethical care platform.

Component 01 Objectives

CTG Scan Interpretation

  • Formulate an image-preprocessing pipeline to digitize thermal paper CTG photos into signal features using OpenCV.
  • Train and benchmark machine learning classifiers to categorize traces into Normal, Mild Risk, and High Risk.
  • Build a secure clinician review and annotation module enabling medical confirmation of AI recommendations.
  • 🎯 Target Target classification accuracy exceeding 90% across validated benchmark datasets.
Component 02 Objectives

Maternal Health Analytics

  • Construct a multi-input risk classifier evaluating blood pressure, blood glucose, heart rate, and CTG outputs.
  • Incorporate SHAP explainability to display contributing feature weights to clinicians and mothers.
  • Design automated push alerting via Firebase Cloud Messaging for HIGH and VERY HIGH risk classifications.
  • 🎯 Target Notification delivery latency under 30 seconds for critical threshold deviations.
Component 03 Objectives

Bilingual Chatbot & Emotional Wellbeing

  • Develop a bilingual Sinhala–English NLP pipeline adapted to pregnancy-related vocabulary and culturally appropriate communication.
  • Implement conversational sentiment detection to adapt chatbot response tone and provide gentle relaxation suggestions.
  • Investigate optional, consent-based, on-device facial expression analysis as supplementary emotional context, while acknowledging potential inaccuracies across users, cameras, and environmental conditions.
  • Conduct a preliminary usability evaluation with the three users who tested the system to identify usability issues and opportunities for improvement.
  • 🎯 Evaluation Goal Assess initial usability and user feedback, with a System Usability Scale (SUS) score above 80 as a target for future evaluation with a larger participant group.
Component 04 Objectives

Virtual Nutritionist & GDM Engine

  • Develop multimodal input mechanisms: voice-assisted logging in Sinhala/EN, YOLOv8 dish recognition, and label OCR.
  • Formulate a pre-consumption GDM risk scoring model evaluating Glycemic Load in context of maternal glucose history.
  • Implement a Gemini-powered "Virtual Chef" generating culturally authentic recipes from available pantry ingredients.
  • 🎯 Target Over 85% mAP in detecting customary Sri Lankan mixed meal items.
Methodology

Design Science Research (DSR) Framework

An iterative engineering research process combining rigorous algorithmic validation with human-centered healthcare ethics.

1. Problem Identification

Systematic literature review and analysis of local antenatal healthcare workflows in Sri Lanka to define technical and operational requirements.

2. Requirements & Ethics

Establishing user personas, functional specifications, data governance protocols, and informed consent boundaries for maternal participants.

3. Architectural Design

Designing the four sub-systems, API schemas, Flutter/React Native client architectures, and Firebase cloud infrastructure.

4. Model Training & Benchmarking

Training computer vision (YOLOv8, OpenCV), machine learning (XGBoost, RF, SVM), and NLP models on curated clinical and public datasets.

5. System Integration

Connecting ML backend services with client mobile interfaces, push notification dispatch, and physician review interfaces.

6. Academic Evaluation

Rigorous performance evaluation (accuracy, precision, recall, F1, latency) and usability assessment (SUS methodology) with relevant academic panels.

Responsible AI & Ethical Governance: All proposed data interactions adhere to strict research guidelines. Participant health data is anonymized. The experimental facial expression analysis (Component 03) requires explicit per-session affirmative opt-in, features a visible on-screen camera indicator, runs exclusively on-device with zero video transmission, and can be disabled with a single tap. All AI components function as non-prescriptive decision support.

COMPONENT 01

AI-Assisted CTG Analysis and Interpretation

Investigator: Thirimanne D.P.B. · Student ID: IT22272386

This component investigates the challenge of making cardiotocography accessible in remote and community environments. In many rural Sri Lankan clinics, mothers receive paper printouts of their non-stress tests without an on-site obstetrician to interpret them.

The proposed system allows smartphone capture of the physical paper trace. Computer vision routines preprocess the image, removing background grid distortions and extracting the FHR baseline, variability, accelerations, and uterine contraction frequency. These features feed a multi-class model outputting a Normal, Mild Risk, or High Risk classification, which is subsequently routed to an assigned clinician for validation.

Photo-based CTG trace digitization utilizing OpenCV adaptive thresholding.
Extraction of FHR baseline, variability, accelerations, and decelerations according to FIGO criteria.
Three-tier risk classification: Normal / Mild Risk / High Risk.
Clinician review portal for confirming, overriding, and annotating AI assessments.

COMPONENT 02

Maternal Health Analytics & Risk Classification

Investigator: Randinu P.V.K.V. · Student ID: IT22557506

Maternal risk is rarely monocausal. This component establishes an integrated risk surveillance engine that correlates biometric data (systolic/diastolic blood pressure, pulse rate, blood glucose, gestational age) with daily patient-reported symptom logs (fetal movement, edema, headache, visual disturbances) and CTG findings from Component 01.

The prediction engine classifies risk into four distinct tiers (LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / VERY HIGH) and generates SHAP-based feature importance plots, enabling clinicians to immediately see which specific physiological markers precipitated an alert.

Ensemble machine learning model benchmarking (XGBoost, Random Forest, SVM).
Four-tier maternal risk stratification: LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / VERY HIGH.
SHAP explainability engine revealing patient-specific risk drivers.
Automated Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) alerts dispatched to assigned clinicians.

COMPONENT 03

Mood-Aware Bilingual Chatbot & Emotional Support System

Investigator: Livera S.D. · Student ID: IT22638168

Emotional wellbeing during pregnancy is an important aspect of maternal healthcare, yet access to affordable, culturally appropriate, and stigma-free support may be limited. This research component explores a bilingual conversational AI system designed to provide supplementary emotional support to expectant mothers in Sinhala and English.

The system uses natural language processing (NLP) to analyse the sentiment of user messages and identify positive, neutral, or distressed language patterns. These estimates can guide the chatbot in generating empathetic, supportive, and context-sensitive responses. An optional facial expression analysis module is investigated as an additional source of emotional context, with attention to informed consent, privacy, and potential inaccuracies across users and camera conditions.

Key Research Features

Bilingual Conversational Support: Sinhala and English communication with culturally appropriate phrasing.
Text Sentiment Analysis: Estimates positive, neutral, or distressed sentiment from conversational messages.
Adaptive Supportive Responses: Adjusts conversational tone according to the estimated sentiment.
Relaxation Support: Explores calming music recommendations, guided breathing exercises, and positive affirmations.
Optional Facial Emotion Analysis: Investigates facial expression-based mood estimation, subject to explicit user consent and privacy safeguards.
Preliminary User Evaluation: Uses feedback from three system testers to identify usability issues and areas for improvement.

COMPONENT 04

Virtual Nutritionist & GDM Action Engine

Investigator: Nuwantha P.A.T. · Student ID: IT22284716

Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM) requires meticulous nutritional vigilance to avert maternal and fetal complications. This component directly tackles dietary tracking fatigue through three low-friction modalities: natural language voice logging, YOLOv8 camera image detection trained on Sri Lankan foods, and nutrition label OCR scanning.

Crucially, the module introduces pre-consumption GDM risk scoring: before eating, the system evaluates the meal's calculated Glycemic Load in context of the mother's recent glucose trends, warning against rapid spikes and suggesting healthier local culinary substitutions via a Gemini-powered "Virtual Chef".

Voice-assisted logging in Sinhala & English + nutrition label OCR.
Computer vision food classification using YOLOv8 on custom Sri Lankan dishes.
Pre-consumption GDM Glycemic Load calculation and suitability warnings.
Gemini-assisted "Virtual Chef" generating pregnancy-safe recipes from home pantry items.
